# Approved project policies for Zackary Brown Web Solutions

Owner-approved for new website projects, confirmed November 16, 2026. The payment, revision, defect-fix, and handover policies are now reflected in the local website source and proposal/onboarding templates. They will appear publicly when the website is deployed. Existing signed agreements remain in force; these policies are not applied retroactively.

The goal is to give buyers clear answers while keeping the $249, $599, and $949 starting packages workable. The approval covers the operating policies below. The signed agreement still needs the project-specific scope and any unresolved legal provisions; approval of these policies does not fill in missing cancellation, intellectual property, or liability terms.

## Client-facing explanation

Before work begins, you receive a written scope with the agreed pages, features, price, timeline, and recurring costs. A 50% deposit reserves the project; the balance is due after approval and before launch or handover.

Your project includes two rounds of consolidated revisions within the agreed scope. A revision round is one collected list of feedback. New pages, features, or a change of direction are priced separately and approved before that work begins. Fixes for work that does not match the agreed scope do not use a revision round.

Your domain and business accounts should remain in your control. After full payment, you receive the agreed website files and access, plus a handover explaining hosting and editing. Third-party software, stock assets, and reusable components remain subject to their licenses. Your proposal identifies any ongoing dependencies or costs.

You have 30 days after launch to report defects against the approved scope. Ongoing edits and maintenance are available separately. A care plan is optional; the scope explains how to move the site and accounts if you choose another provider.

## Items to settle in the signed agreement

- Identify the contracting parties and business/trading name accurately.
- Define cancellation, work completed, unused prepaid amounts, and any nonrecoverable third-party expenses. Do not call every deposit automatically nonrefundable without reviewing the agreement and applicable law.
- List the exact assets and rights being transferred. Payment or possession of website files alone does not establish a copyright assignment. U.S. law generally requires a signed writing for a copyright ownership transfer; see [17 U.S.C. § 204](https://www.copyright.gov/title17/92chap2.html#204).
- Separate original custom work, the client's existing content, reusable developer tools, open-source dependencies, stock licenses, and AI-assisted material. Promise only the rights you can provide.
- State whether portfolio screenshots, links, and client names may be published. Get separate permission for testimonials and measured results.
- Set any maintenance edit allowance, response window, monitoring, backups and restoration scope, cancellation notice, and migration assistance. The current $49 plan needs a practical boundary before adding stronger guarantees.
- Address accessibility scope and ongoing responsibilities without promising universal certification or guaranteed legal compliance.
- Exclude guaranteed search positions, traffic, sales, and revenue. Define the actual SEO setup delivered and how any ongoing work is measured.
- Review confidentiality, data access, liability, dispute resolution, and jurisdiction with qualified counsel. Do not copy another agency's legal clauses into your agreement without review.
